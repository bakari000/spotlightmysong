Cover art review

What works

The silhouette against the bright window is strong. We shrank it down to the size people see while scrolling, and the shape still reads clearly. It feels mysterious and fits the underground lane.

What could be better

  • The shadows are crushed. About a third of the image is pure black, so the hoodie detail disappears.
  • There is no artist name or title on it, so anyone who sees it on its own won't know who it is.
  • There's a small smudge in the sky near the top left.

What we did

Since the song is out, keep the cover as is. Your promo graphics lift the shadows, clean the smudge, and put 2MCH and Ball front and center so every post builds your name.

Next time

Upload covers at 3000 by 3000 pixels, and add your name somewhere small so the art works on its own.

Post at the times your followers are most active. Instagram shows this under Insights. If you're not sure, evenings between 6 and 9 are a good start.

After the two weeks

This is how labels keep an artist moving between drops. Do the same and every release starts stronger than the last.

  1. Day 14: check your numbers. In Spotify for Artists, look at listeners, saves and where they came from. Note which post brought people in.
  2. Weeks 3 to 6: keep the song alive. Post two or three times a week using what worked. A song does not stop being new just because release week ended.
  3. Week 4: lock in the next drop. Pick your next song and a Friday release date six to eight weeks out.
  4. Week 5: upload early. Get the next song to your distributor three to four weeks before release so you can pitch it to Spotify's editors.
  5. Every drop: same playbook, better each time. Releasing on a steady schedule is what builds a fanbase. One song rarely does it alone.
